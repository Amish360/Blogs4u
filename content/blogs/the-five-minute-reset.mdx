Most stress-management advice assumes you have time you don't actually have. Meditate for twenty minutes. Journal every night. Take a long walk. These are good practices, but they're also easy to skip on the days you need them most.

## Why five minutes works

The goal of the five-minute reset isn't deep relaxation — it's interrupting the stress response before it stacks on top of the next thing you have to do. A short break between a hard meeting and the next task gives your nervous system a chance to downshift instead of carrying tension forward all day.

## The routine

1. **One minute of box breathing.** Inhale for four counts, hold for four, exhale for four, hold for four. Repeat six times.
2. **Two minutes of movement.** Stand up, roll your shoulders back, stretch your arms overhead, and walk to another room and back if you can. Movement clears cortisol faster than sitting still does.
3. **One minute of naming.** Silently name three things you can see and one thing you're grateful for, however small. This pulls your attention out of the last meeting and into the present room.
4. **One minute of intention.** Before opening your next task, decide the single most important thing to accomplish in the next hour.

## Why this beats a coffee break

A coffee break gives your body caffeine; the five-minute reset gives your nervous system an actual signal that the stressful thing is over. Over weeks, doing this consistently — even imperfectly — tends to lower the baseline tension that builds across a workday, so evenings feel less like recovery and more like an extension of a normal day.

## Make it sustainable

Don't wait for a bad day to try this. Attach it to something you already do — right after a meeting ends, right before lunch — so it becomes automatic rather than one more thing to remember.

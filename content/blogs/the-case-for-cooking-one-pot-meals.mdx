There's a particular kind of kitchen exhaustion that comes from cooking a technically impressive meal on a Tuesday night — three pans, a timer for each component, a sink full of dishes before you've even sat down to eat. One-pot meals solve a problem most recipes don't bother acknowledging: cooking on a weeknight is a logistics problem as much as a culinary one.

## Flavor actually improves

This isn't just about convenience. When rice cooks in the same pot that browned your onions and garlic, it picks up every bit of fond left on the bottom. A braise that simmers meat, vegetables, and aromatics together for an hour develops a depth that's hard to replicate by cooking components separately and combining them at the end. Constraint, in this case, produces better food.

## A basic formula that works for almost anything

1. Brown your protein or aromatics first, directly in the pot you'll finish cooking in
2. Add vegetables that take longer to cook — root vegetables, hardy greens — before anything delicate
3. Add your liquid and starch (rice, lentils, pasta) together, so the starch absorbs flavor as it cooks
4. Finish with anything that only needs a few minutes: herbs, leafy greens, a squeeze of citrus

## A few favorites worth trying

- A Spanish-style one-pot rice with chorizo, peppers, and smoked paprika
- Coconut lentil dal with spinach stirred in at the end
- A chicken and orzo braise finished with lemon and dill

## The real win is timing

The best part of one-pot cooking isn't the single dish to wash — it's that most of these recipes tolerate an extra ten minutes on the stove without ruining dinner. That forgiveness is worth more on a weeknight than any garnish.

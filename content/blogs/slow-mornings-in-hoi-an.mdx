I arrived in Hoi An expecting to stay four days. I left three weeks later, and I still think about going back.

## The lantern town at dawn

Everyone photographs Hoi An's Ancient Town at night, when the silk lanterns glow over the Thu Bon River and the streets fill with tourists. But the town has a completely different personality at six in the morning, before the shops open. Fishermen unload the night's catch at the market, women sweep the steps of French colonial shopfronts, and the light coming off the river is soft enough to make anything look good.

## What I actually did all day

Mostly nothing, on purpose. I rented a bicycle for the entire stay and rode out past the rice paddies toward An Bang Beach most afternoons, stopping wherever looked interesting. A few habits formed:

- Coffee at the same corner stall every morning, where the owner started remembering my order by day three
- Long, aimless rides through the countryside with no destination in mind
- Evening swims at An Bang, followed by grilled seafood at a plastic-table restaurant with no sign

## A lesson in slowing down

I've traveled fast for most of my career — three cities in a week, a camera bag that never gets fully unpacked. Hoi An taught me something different. Staying in one place long enough to recognize faces, to have a "usual" coffee order, to notice how the light changes across a single week rather than a single afternoon — it produces a different kind of photograph, and a different kind of memory.

## If you go

Skip the resorts on the beach road and stay in the old quarter instead, close enough to walk everywhere. Rent a bike immediately. And resist the urge to fill every day with a plan — the best afternoons I had were the ones I didn't schedule at all.
